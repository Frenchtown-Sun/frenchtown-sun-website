Borough Council to Vote on Prohibiting Data Centers in Frenchtown

On Wednesday, October 7, Borough Council voted in favor of introducing Ordinance #963, which would amend borough codes to prohibit data centers in all zones of the borough. The Council’s action followed recommendations provided by the Planning Board that were prepared at the request of Mayor Brad Myhre. In a September 2, 2026, press release, Executive Director of the Association of New Jersey Environmental Commissions was quoted as saying, “To date, at least 109 New Jersey municipalities and an entire county have taken action to permanently or temporarily ban data centers.” The Council’s action was prompted by a combination of factors: concerns expressed by borough residents regarding environmental impacts and electricity costs, actions already taken by neighboring towns (Alexandria, Kingwood and Milford) to ban or restrict data centers, and an ongoing national conversation about data centers.

Under the New Jersey Municipal Land Use Law, municipalities are authorized to identify permitted uses within various zones—such as residences, retail establishments, gas stations and schools. Although data centers are not currently listed as a permitted use under borough rules, rendering them disallowed by default, Borough Professional Planner Darlene Green noted that developers in other communities have circumvented local zoning by housing data centers inside warehouses. Borough Attorney Albert Cruz explained at a Council meeting that explicitly defining and prohibiting data centers provides a clear legal safeguard against such workarounds. Furthermore, the Planning Board carefully crafted the ordinance to ensure that it would not be deemed “arbitrary and capricious” in the event of a legal challenge. A key priority for both the Planning Board in its recommendations and the Borough Council in its October 7 action was addressing a pre-existing facility owned by Brightspeed. Because the facility provides phone and internet service, the ordinance ensures the facility would not be classified as a data center.

After the introduction of the ordinance at the October Borough Council meeting, the draft ordinance will return to the Planning Board for a “consistency review” to ensure alignment with the town’s Master Plan. If the Planning Board finds that the ordinance is “not inconsistent” with the master plan, the ordinance will be returned to Borough Council for a public hearing at the Council’s November meeting after which Council will vote to either adopt or reject the ordinance. If adopted, the ordinance will go into effect and become part of Frenchtown’s municipal code.

The draft ordinance is available on the Borough website at: frenchtownboro.com/local-government/legal-notices/.
